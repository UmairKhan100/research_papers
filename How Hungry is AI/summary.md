This research paper introduces a comprehensive framework to measure the environmental impact of large language model inference, focusing on energy consumption, water usage, and carbon emissions.

By analyzing 30 prominent models, the authors reveal that while individual queries may seem efficient, the global scale of AI deployment drives massive resource depletion.

The study highlights a significant performance-to-sustainability paradox, noting that high-reasoning models like **o3** and **DeepSeek-R1** are remarkably resource-intensive compared to more eco-efficient options like **Claude-3.7 Sonnet**.

Through a case study of **GPT-4o**, the text illustrates that annual AI operations can consume as much electricity as tens of thousands of homes and evaporate enough freshwater to meet the drinking needs of millions.

Ultimately, the sources argue that infrastructure and deployment hardware are just as critical as model architecture in determining the true ecological cost of artificial intelligence.
